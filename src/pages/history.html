01Our History

Five homes in
fifteen years.

Before Tower settled at 13 Prospect Avenue in 1917, it moved between four different houses. This is the story of how a small group of students started a club that has lasted for generations.

Founded 1902
13 Prospect since 1917

—Spring, 1902

In the spring of 1902, four sophomores and Frank “Pop” Little ’03 decided to start a new upper-class club. They put down $400 that April, and by September Tower had twenty-six members.

02The Timeline

How we
got here.

1902 Spring

A new club

Four sophomores and Frank “Pop” Little ’03 proposed a new upper-class eating club and placed a $400 stake that April.

1902

The Monastery

Our first home was a modest house on University Place known as “the Monastery.” The first members were thirteen seniors and thirteen juniors.

1903

“The Incubator”

When Charter moved out of Gulick House on Olden Street, Tower moved in for about a year.

1904

89 Prospect

Tower moved into Cottage’s former clubhouse, built in 1892 and moved down the street when Cottage built its new home. It came with hot-air heating, a sidewalk and a tennis court.

1911

The Fine House

Tower moved to Nassau Street, into the Fine House, which had been Quadrangle’s clubhouse.

1915

A home of our own

Having outgrown the Fine House, Tower asked Roderic Barnes ’03 to design a permanent clubhouse between Campus and Cannon.

1917

13 Prospect Avenue

Our clubhouse was finished in 1917, with the tower and the club seal at its center. It has been our home ever since.

1921 Fire

Fire in the tower

A fire broke out in the tower. It was mostly contained there, and the club made repairs quickly.

War years Wartime

Open through the war

Even when membership fell to fifteen ROTC officers, Tower kept its doors open.

Today Growing

Still home

Tower helped found the Interclub Council and the Prospect Foundation, and the house has grown with a sunroom, a billiards room and new kitchens. 220 members call it home today.

04The Architecture

Built to be
lived in.

Barnes drew on the brick Collegiate Gothic buildings nearby, like Campus Club, Cap & Gown and 1879 Hall. He placed a square tower at the center of the house, and it became the club’s emblem.

One member wrote that the goal was “an ample, attractive, and practical clubhouse without making it ostentatious.” More than a century later, it still feels that way.
